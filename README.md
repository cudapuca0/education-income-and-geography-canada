## Why I Built This Project

We're often told that education is the most reliable path to economic opportunity. But I began wondering whether that story is equally true everywhere.

Does a degree lead to the same financial outcomes in downtown Toronto as it does in a smaller community? Do certain regions reward education differently? Does geography shape opportunity just as much as education itself?

This project explores those questions using data from the 2021 Canadian Census.

---

## Research Question

**Does education translate into higher income equally across metropolitan and non-metropolitan areas in Canada?**

Using national census microdata, this project explores how geography influences income outcomes and whether metropolitan areas provide additional economic advantages beyond education alone.

---

## Data Source

**Statistics Canada – 2021 Census Public Use Microdata File (PUMF)**

Variables analyzed include:

- After-tax income
- Field of study
- Geographic classification
- Population weights

All statistics and visualizations were calculated using census weights to ensure population-representative estimates.

## Key Questions Explored

### Does Geography Affect Income?

The analysis compares income distributions between metropolitan and non-metropolitan Canada.

While differences are relatively small at lower income levels, the gap becomes increasingly visible toward the top of the income distribution.

This suggests that metropolitan areas create additional opportunities for higher earners.

### Does the Return to Education Depend on Field of Study?

Some fields benefit substantially more from metropolitan labor markets than others.

Fields with some of the largest metropolitan income premiums include:

- Business & Public Administration
- Social Sciences & Law
- Mathematics & Computer Science

Meanwhile, fields such as Education and Agriculture show much smaller location-based differences.

### Does the Metropolitan Advantage Vary Across Canada?

The project also explores metropolitan income premiums by province.

The results suggest that Canada is not a single unified labor market. Instead, regional economies create different opportunities and rewards depending on location.

---

## Key Findings

### Education Matters

Higher levels of education are generally associated with higher income across Canada.

### Geography Matters Too

The economic return to education is not identical everywhere.

The same degree may generate different outcomes depending on the labor market, industries, and opportunities available in a region.

### Metropolitan Advantage Increases at Higher Incomes

The income gap between metropolitan and non-metropolitan areas becomes much larger near the top of the income distribution.

This suggests that cities disproportionately benefit higher earners.

### Opportunity Is Spatial

Perhaps the most important insight from this project is that opportunity is shaped by geography.

Education provides skills and credentials, but regional economic structures influence how those skills are rewarded.
